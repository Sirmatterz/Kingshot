🔍 Where your damage comes from

Build each march you'll actually send. Governor gear, charms, research and pets are account-wide and live in My Setup — what changes here is which heroes go on which march, their hero gear, host vs join, and the formation. Most players run 5–6 marches; set them up once and this becomes your bear-day checklist.

🔁 How many marches will you actually land?

This is the number that matters most and the one people underestimate. A hosted rally ties a slot up for the whole gather countdown; a join recycles as fast as you can travel. Players who join aggressively land 50–60 marches in a single bear, and 100+ when the alliance keeps rallies open. Set your target, spread it across your loadouts, then tune each one.

Or set the “× sent” box on each march by hand — useful when one loadout is your fast joiner and another is a slow hosted rally.

🎒 Troops you own vs troops you're committing

Every formation here is capped by what you actually have. If a type runs out it becomes the binding constraint and the optimiser fills the gap with whatever else you own — which is why the "ideal" 1/10/89 is usually unreachable. Set your real counts in My Setup → Troops You Own.

Why march count is the biggest lever you have. Damage scales with the square root of the troops in a march, but every march fights its own full 10 rounds. Two consequences: (1) splitting a fixed army across M marches gives ≈√M × the damage — 4 marches of 25k beat 1 march of 100k by ~100%. (2) if you can retrain and refill between cycles, each extra march is a whole extra battle — linear, even better. Either way, marches and cycles buy damage more cheaply than almost anything you can upgrade.

⏱️ Alliance rally cadence

The single biggest throughput lever isn't your account — it's how the alliance spaces its rally launches. Stagger them and a returning march never waits: launch one every S minutes and your average wait is only S/2. Sustaining that cadence needs enough hosts in rotation, which is why a first row of 5–6 hosts works.

Hosting makes your marches sit through the full gather countdown before they launch. Joining lets you fire the moment a slot opens. Since your own troops are the same either way, this is purely a question of how many marches you can land in the window.

Turn this from an estimate into a measurement. Record what you actually sent at a bear and the score you actually got. The app then works out which damage model the game really uses, and scales its constants to match your account.

One run isn't enough — any model can be scaled to hit a single number. To tell them apart you need two or more runs that differ: different total troop counts (tests the √ shape) or different formations (tests per-type vs whole-army).
You don't need a bear for this. A 30-minute bear is a terrible lab — you can't afford to send a deliberately small march just to measure something. So use a Beast or Terror instead: hit them on demand, as many times as you like, with whatever troop counts you want.

It works because the target only contributes a constant multiplier. The √ shape and the ratios between troop tiers are properties of your army, so they carry straight over to the bear. Only the absolute scale is target-specific, and the app fits a separate one for each target — so you can mix bear runs and beast runs in the same table and it still reaches one verdict.

🪖 Measure troop base attack directly

The decisive experiment. Send two marches of the same troop count but different tiers — say 30,000 Apex archers and 30,000 Elite archers — with everything else identical. Every term in the damage formula cancels except base attack, so the ratio of the two scores is the ratio of their base attack. Record both above, tag each with its tier, and this measures what no website publishes.
